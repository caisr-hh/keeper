# FEEL-AI

## Project overview

FEEL-AI (**Federated Predictive Models on Edge for the Logistics Industry**) was a research and innovation project
exploring how artificial intelligence can be developed and used across distributed industrial systems in the logistics
sector. Coordinated by Halmstad University in collaboration with Volvo Group Trucks Technology and Toyota Material
Handling Europe, the project investigated federated learning for predictive maintenance: enabling industrial assets and
organisations to contribute to shared machine-learning models while reducing the need to collect raw data in one central
location.

The work combined real industrial data, predictive modelling, federated and collaborative learning, explainability, and
research into differences between customers, vehicles, and operating environments.

## Motivation and objectives

Industrial vehicles and machines generate operational data that can support predictive maintenance and other predictive
services. Collecting all such data centrally may be costly, slow, or undesirable because of privacy and confidentiality
concerns. FEEL-AI therefore explored predictive modelling across distributed industrial systems, particularly in
heavy-duty vehicle and material-handling settings.

The project's objectives were to:

- Investigate federated learning for industrial predictive-maintenance applications.
- Reduce dependence on centralised collection and transfer of raw industrial data.
- Study how differences in data availability and operating conditions affect collaborative learning.
- Investigate ways to make model behaviour easier to understand.
- Connect machine-learning research with real industrial data and operational requirements.

## Research and development

The original project vision centred on federated learning. As the consortium worked with industrial data, it also
addressed the foundations needed to make distributed and shared models useful in practice: data access and quality,
repeatable modelling workflows, and differences between customers and operating environments. Research on federated and
collaborative learning continued alongside this work, including investigations of model merging, explainability, and
robustness.

### Industrial data and modelling workflows

The partners established access to relevant industrial data and worked on understanding and preparing data from
vehicles, customers, and different operating environments. They developed more systematic, reproducible approaches for
data preparation, predictive-model training, comparison of alternative methods, and experiment management.

### Heterogeneous and collaborative learning

The project examined how differences in customer environments, data availability, and operating conditions affect the
performance and reuse of predictive models. Research addressed federated learning and related approaches for sharing
knowledge across distributed industrial systems, extending to model merging and other forms of collaborative learning.

### Explainability and robustness

FEEL-AI investigated how predictive-model behaviour can be understood more clearly and how models can respond to
conditions not represented during training.

### Project organisation

The work was organised into four work packages:

1. Project management and knowledge sharing.
1. Industrial data access.
1. Federated analytics and research.
1. Demonstration and deployment-oriented activities.

## Outcomes and impact

FEEL-AI combined industrial collaboration, engineering work, and scientific research. Its reported outcomes include:

- Experience with industrial data access and preparation shared among the partners.
- Reusable modelling and experimentation workflows.
- Practical understanding of data quality and differences across customers and operating conditions.
- Research contributions in heterogeneous learning, federated and collaborative learning, explainability, model merging,
  and robustness to previously unseen conditions.
- Published and submitted research outputs and wider knowledge-sharing activities.
- Contributions to the **SOMI 2026** workshop and tutorial at ECML PKDD 2026.
- A foundation for continued collaboration and research through the follow-up **UNIFY-AI** project.

A broader lesson from the collaboration was that useful industrial AI requires attention not only to model selection but
also to data, experimentation, evaluation, operational context, and deployment constraints.

## Partners and collaboration

### Halmstad University — CAISR

The **Center for Applied Intelligent Systems Research (CAISR)** at Halmstad University coordinated FEEL-AI and led the
machine-learning research. Its work covered predictive modelling, federated and collaborative learning, heterogeneous
data, explainability, and robustness, informed by industrial data and use cases from the consortium.

### Volvo Group Trucks Technology

**Volvo Group Trucks Technology (VGTT)** represented the heavy-duty vehicle domain, contributing industrial data, domain
knowledge, and operational perspectives on predictive modelling and data-driven services for commercial vehicles.

### Toyota Material Handling Europe

**Toyota Material Handling Europe (TMHE)** represented the material-handling domain, providing industrial data and
experience from forklift and customer environments. Data from different customers supported investigations into
variation across industrial settings.

Together, the partners connected machine-learning research with operational requirements and the practical conditions
under which models must work across fleets, customers, and organisations.

### SOMI 2026

Experience from FEEL-AI contributed to establishing **Systems Over Models: What Actually Works in Industry (SOMI
2026)**, a workshop and tutorial at **ECML PKDD 2026**. SOMI provided a forum for discussing practical challenges in
industrial machine-learning systems and sharing lessons beyond the FEEL-AI consortium.

## Continued research: UNIFY-AI

Research questions, technical foundations, and collaboration developed during FEEL-AI contributed to the follow-up
[**UNIFY-AI**](unify-ai.md) project. UNIFY-AI continues collaboration between Halmstad University and Volvo research
groups, focusing on time-series foundation models and reusable representations for industrial applications.
